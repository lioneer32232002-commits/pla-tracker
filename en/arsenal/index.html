What These Sales Tell Us

Placing Taiwan in the international context of who the US sells to, and how much, shows what tier of weapon the US is willing to hand Taiwan. Each reading below cites the peer-buyer comparison data.

F-16V — the world’s largest single buyer

Taiwan is buying 66 newly built F-16 Block 70/72 jets — the largest single buyer worldwide, ahead of Turkey (40), Morocco (25), Bulgaria and Bahrain (16 each). Selling the latest F-16 and letting Taiwan be the top buyer is itself a marker of capability and trust.

DSCA

Harpoon — an order no one else matches

Taiwan’s 460 Harpoon missiles (400 coastal-defense in 100 launcher sets, plus 60 air-launched) dwarf every other buyer (India 43, Egypt 20, South Korea 19, Philippines 12) — the largest single Harpoon order in years, and the core weapon for anti-invasion defense.

DSCA·Archive

HIMARS — the world’s second-largest buyer

Taiwan’s 111 HIMARS launchers rank second worldwide, behind only Poland (506) and ahead of Australia (90) and Romania (54). HIMARS proved the value of mobile precision fires in Ukraine.

DSCA

Javelin — second only to Poland among paying buyers

Taiwan’s ~1,810 Javelin missiles rank second among Foreign Military Sales buyers, behind Poland (2,506) and ahead of Australia (1,308); Ukraine’s 10,000+ are US military aid and counted separately.

DSCA

ATACMS — a marker of the trust tier

The December 2025 HIMARS package (case 26-01) includes 420 M57 ATACMS — deep-strike missiles with roughly 300 km range. Willingness to sell a weapon that can strike deep across the strait signals a higher tier of trust in Taiwan. This is our own contextual reading, not an official position: the only citable document is the DSCA notice itself, and Washington has made no corresponding statement about a "trust tier".

Scope & CaveatsThis page tracks US Foreign Military Sales (FMS) to Taiwan notified to Congress by the Defense Security Cooperation Agency (DSCA) since 2019. A notification is not a signed contract, and neither is a delivery. A DSCA notice is the congressional-notification stage; the actual Letter of Offer and Acceptance (LOA) and delivery schedule come later. Direct commercial sales and US-forces transfers are excluded. Values are notified ceilings; signed amounts are often lower. Sources are official DSCA notices and web-archive snapshots, with delivery progress cross-checked against CNA / MND / defense-trade press, compiled case by case with a source for each. Weapon photos are US Department of Defense DVIDS archive images (public domain, photographer credited on each weapon page); thumbnails depict the same system type as a representative image, not equipment as delivered to Taiwan.
